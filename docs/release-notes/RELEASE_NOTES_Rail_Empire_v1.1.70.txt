Rail Empire v1.1.70 — Zero-Margin Vmax / Timetable Pacing Fix
2026-08-19

FIELD REPORT
- V2 schedules still looked too relaxed in real operation.
- Trains often failed to reach their permitted Vmax even on clear line, despite automatic timetable margin being fixed at 0 s.
- Screenshot/field observation showed an on-line passenger train cruising around 89 km/h with no infrastructure/signal reason to do so.

ROOT CAUSE
- A timetable regulator introduced in v1.1.44 converted remaining distance / remaining booked time into a required AVERAGE speed.
- That average was then incorrectly applied as an INSTANTANEOUS speed cap.
- Example consequence: a 160 km/h train whose optimal run averages ~90 km/h because of acceleration/braking could be capped near 90 km/h for most of the trip, so it never reached Vmax.
- This contradicted the V2 zero-margin contract. The timetable calculator itself already adds no automatic margin.

FIX
- V2 zero-margin schedules no longer apply any timetable-derived cruise speed cap.
- En-route speed is now determined only by train Vmax, infrastructure speed, train-length speed transitions, signalling/cantons, traffic conflicts, works/incidents, electrification, weather and physical acceleration/braking.
- Existing physical timing calculation remains second-precise with automaticMarginSec = 0.
- Passenger no-advance protection remains at the booked arrival boundary: if numerical/runtime differences make a train reach the exact route endpoint early, it waits there until booked arrival instead of being slowed over the whole leg.
- Late trains are likewise never slowed by timetable pacing.

UNCHANGED / REGRESSION PROTECTION
- v1.1.69 long-route stability and multi-target routing retained.
- 10/14 robust cursor candidates retained.
- Unknown FALLBACK_30 remains a data warning rather than a hidden 30 km/h timetable restriction.
- Real documented OSM speed limits remain authoritative.
- No synthetic straight-line routing fallback.
- Incident scheduler/icons/delay reasons, Works V2, rame duplication, frequency duplication and schedule search retained.
